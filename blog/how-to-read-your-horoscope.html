How to Read Your Horoscope: A Beginner's Guide to Your Zodiac Sign

By The Selene Team· 4 October 2026· 9 min read
AstrologyZodiacBeginners

Most people know their star sign — but a horoscope is far richer than a single line in a magazine. Once you understand your sun, moon and rising signs and the four elements, you can read your horoscope in a way that's genuinely useful for reflection. Here's how to start.

✨ Key takeaways

  • Your sun sign is your core self; your moon sign is your emotions; your rising sign is how others see you.
  • There are 12 zodiac signs grouped into four elements: fire, earth, air and water.
  • Reading for your rising sign often makes horoscopes feel more accurate.
  • Use a horoscope as a daily nudge for reflection, not a prediction.

What is a horoscope?

A horoscope is an interpretation of the positions of the sun, moon and planets in relation to the twelve zodiac signs. A daily horoscope zooms in on how current movements might relate to your sign; a full birth chart maps the whole sky at the exact moment you were born. Astrology isn't a science — think of a horoscope as a thoughtful prompt that helps you check in with yourself.

Your “big three” signs

If you only read your sun sign, you're missing most of the picture. Astrologers talk about the “big three”:

  • Sun sign — your core identity, ego and personality. Set by your birth date.
  • Moon sign — your inner emotional world and instincts. Needs your birth date and time.
  • Rising sign (ascendant) — how you come across and first appear to others. Needs your exact birth time and place.

Reading your horoscope for both your sun and rising sign is a simple upgrade that makes it feel far more personal.

The four elements

The twelve signs fall into four elemental groups that describe their basic temperament:

  • Fire (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius) — passionate, energetic, bold.
  • Earth (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn) — grounded, practical, reliable.
  • Air (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius) — curious, social, intellectual.
  • Water (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces) — emotional, intuitive, deep.

Daily, weekly, monthly and yearly horoscopes

Different timeframes serve different purposes:

  • Daily — a quick mood and intention check for the day ahead.
  • Weekly — themes to watch across work, love and energy.
  • Monthly — bigger shifts and good windows for plans.
  • Yearly — the broad story and major turning points of the year.

How to actually use your horoscope

  1. Read for your sun and rising signs. Together they're more accurate than the sun alone.
  2. Look for the theme, not the fine print. Note the overall mood and message.
  3. Turn it into one intention. “Today I'll slow down and listen” is more useful than a prediction.
  4. Reflect at day's end. Did the theme show up? Journaling builds real self-awareness.
  5. Hold it lightly. Let a horoscope prompt reflection, never dictate decisions.

Don't forget the moon

The moon moves quickly and sets the emotional tone. A new moon is a natural time to set intentions and start fresh; a full moon is a time of culmination, release and heightened feeling. Tracking the moon phase alongside your horoscope adds a gentle rhythm to the month.
