How to Read Tarot Cards: A Beginner's Guide to All 78 Cards

By The Selene Team· 4 October 2026· 10 min read
TarotBeginnersCard meanings

Reading tarot is far simpler than it looks. Once you understand how the 78-card deck is built, you can give a thoughtful, meaningful reading on your very first day — no memorising, and no special powers required. This guide walks you through the deck, the card meanings and three easy spreads you can use right now.

✨ Key takeaways

  • A tarot deck has 78 cards: 22 Major Arcana (life's big themes) and 56 Minor Arcana (everyday life).
  • The Minor Arcana is split into four suits — Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles.
  • You don't need to memorise every card; learn the patterns and reason the rest out.
  • Start with a one-card draw or a three-card spread.
  • Tarot is a tool for reflection, not fortune-telling.

What is a tarot reading?

A tarot reading is a conversation between you, a question and a set of symbolic cards. You shuffle the deck while focusing on a question, lay out a few cards in set positions (a “spread”), and interpret what each card means in that position. The images are prompts for reflection: they help you look at a situation from angles you might not have considered.

Importantly, tarot does not tell you a fixed future. It mirrors what you already sense, surfaces hidden feelings, and offers perspective. The value is in the thinking it unlocks — which is exactly why it works so well as a daily habit for clarity and calm.

The 78-card deck explained

Nearly every modern deck follows the structure of the Rider–Waite–Smith deck, first published in 1909 and now in the public domain. All 78 cards fall into two groups:

GroupCardsCovers
Major Arcana22Major life themes, turning points, your bigger journey
Minor Arcana56Everyday events, feelings, people and practical matters

When a reading is full of Major Arcana cards, the moment feels significant and fated. When it's mostly Minor Arcana, you're looking at the ordinary, day-to-day texture of life.

The Major Arcana: life's big story

The 22 Major Arcana cards run from 0 — The Fool to XXI — The World. Read in order, they tell the story of a journey from innocent beginnings, through challenges and lessons, to wholeness. This is often called “the Fool's Journey.”

You don't need to memorise all 22 at once. A few anchors help:

  • The Fool (0) — new beginnings, a leap of faith.
  • The Lovers (VI) — relationships, choices, alignment of values.
  • The Wheel of Fortune (X) — change, cycles, luck turning.
  • The Tower (XVI) — sudden upheaval, truth breaking through.
  • The Star (XVII) — hope, healing, renewed faith.
  • The World (XXI) — completion, achievement, coming full circle.

The Minor Arcana & the four suits

The 56 Minor Arcana cards work like a playing-card deck: four suits of 14 cards each (Ace through Ten, plus four court cards — Page, Knight, Queen and King). Each suit maps to an element and a theme:

SuitElementTheme
WandsFireEnergy, passion, creativity, drive
CupsWaterEmotions, love, relationships, intuition
SwordsAirThoughts, truth, conflict, decisions
PentaclesEarthMoney, work, home, the physical world

Numbers add a second layer. Aces are beginnings; the middle numbers show development; Tens are completion. So the Ace of Cups is the start of an emotional chapter, while the Ten of Pentacles is lasting material security. Learn the four suits and the number pattern and you can interpret any of the 56 cards — that's the shortcut that replaces memorising.

How to do your first reading, step by step

  1. Set a clear question. Open questions work best — “What should I focus on this week?” beats a yes/no question.
  2. Shuffle while you focus. Hold your question in mind as you shuffle. Stop when it feels right.
  3. Draw and lay out your cards. Pick a spread (see below) and place the cards face up.
  4. Read each card in its position. Note your first impression of the image, then add the traditional meaning.
  5. Tell the story. Connect the cards into one narrative that answers your question.

Three beginner-friendly spreads

1. The single card

One card for a daily focus or a quick question. Perfect for building confidence — just draw and reflect.

2. The three-card spread

The workhorse of tarot. Lay three cards left to right and read them as Past → Present → Future, or Situation → Action → Outcome. Simple, flexible and genuinely insightful.

3. The Celtic Cross

A classic ten-card spread covering the heart of a situation, what crosses it, the past, the near future, hopes and fears, and the likely outcome. It's more advanced — come back to it once three-card readings feel natural.

Upright vs reversed cards

If a card appears upside down, it's reversed. A reversal usually softens, blocks or turns the upright meaning inward. For example, upright The Sun is open joy; reversed, it can be quieter happiness or a temporary cloud. Reversals are entirely optional — many readers start with upright-only meanings and add reversals once the upright cards feel familiar.

Tips for better, more honest readings

  • Trust your first impression. Your instinctive reaction to an image is part of the reading.
  • Keep a tarot journal. Note the cards, your question and what happened. Patterns emerge fast.
  • Read the cards together, not in isolation — they comment on each other.
  • Ask better questions. “How can I…” and “What should I know about…” open more doors than “Will I…”
  • Stay grounded. Use readings for insight, not life-altering decisions.
